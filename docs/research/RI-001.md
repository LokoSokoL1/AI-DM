# RI-001 — Trust Through Persistence, Perspective, and World Independence

- **Status:** Accepted
- **System:** Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition (2014)
- **Adventure:** Lost Mine of Phandelver
- **Scope:** Goblin Ambush through the party's first long rest in Phandalin, ending on the morning of Day 3

## Objective

Identify behaviours that increase or reduce player trust in an AI Dungeon Master and derive permanent design and architectural requirements.

Trust is evaluated through rules consistency, world consistency, NPC consistency, perspective integrity, consequence persistence, and fair adjudication.

## Campaign State Snapshot

- Nekria travelled with Brom, Father Garrick, and Elara.
- The party survived the Goblin Ambush and entered Cragmaw Hideout.
- Sildar was rescued.
- Gundren had been taken toward Cragmaw Castle.
- Negotiation with Yeemik changed the hideout's power structure and removed Klarg from control.
- The party reached Phandalin, began meeting its inhabitants, gathered rumours and leads, and completed its first long rest there.

## Overall Assessment

The campaign was highly successful overall. Combat, exploration, and social interaction functioned naturally. Most weaknesses were not mechanical; they involved perspective leakage, missing persistence, over-optimization, and insufficient world simulation. A trustworthy DM depends more on judgement and memory than on storytelling ability.

## Observations

### 1 — Perspective Integrity

#### Observation

Several NPCs demonstrated knowledge they had never legitimately acquired:

- Toblen addressed Brom by name despite Nekria introducing only herself.
- Qelline referred to Gundren after Nekria mentioned only “our friend.”
- Stonehill Inn was described as “familiar” on the party's first visit.
- Sildar awkwardly corrected “Gundren” to “your friend” despite personally knowing Gundren.

#### Confidence

Very High

#### Analysis

These were not ordinary memory failures. Module, narrator, NPC, and player knowledge had become blurred. Knowledge belongs to a specific perspective and requires a legitimate acquisition path.

#### Design Principle

Perspective Integrity.

#### Requirement

Keep canonical adventure information, established campaign facts, narrator knowledge, individual NPC knowledge, player knowledge, rumours, suspicions, and beliefs separate.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires perspective-isolated knowledge records or graphs with owner, source, acquisition history, confidence or epistemic type, and controlled access. This is not an implementation claim.

#### Validation

Replay affected scenes while preventing NPCs from accessing knowledge outside their perspective. Dialogue must not use unavailable facts; behaviour may change only after information is legitimately acquired.

### 2 — Canonical vs Established Reality

#### Observation

Some assumptions came from the published module rather than from events established in this campaign. For example, the adventure may later imply that Toblen and Sildar are acquainted, but the campaign had not established that relationship.

#### Confidence

Very High

#### Analysis

The module is not the campaign. Canon is source material; campaign truth is created through play.

#### Design Principle

World Independence and Perspective Integrity.

#### Requirement

Maintain separate records for Canonical Truth and Established Campaign Truth. Canonical information may influence play only after it has legitimately entered the campaign.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires a canonical-source layer separate from authoritative campaign state, with explicit promotion or establishment events and provenance.

#### Validation

Load canonical information that has not entered play and verify that it does not affect NPC behaviour or narration as an established fact. After a legitimate establishment event, verify that the same fact may be used with its provenance intact.

### 3 — Player Journal

#### Observation

As information accumulated, players benefited from recalling people, rumours, leads, unfinished business, locations, and objectives.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Human players often keep notes. Dungeon Manager should assist memory without replacing investigation or exposing hidden state.

#### Design Principle

Perspective Integrity and Information Should Be Earned.

#### Requirement

Maintain a player-facing journal containing only legitimately known information, separated into Facts, Rumours, Claims, Suspicions, Questions, and Objectives.

Examples: Fact: Sildar was rescued. Rumour: Smoke was seen in Neverwinter Wood. Claim: Narth's uncle believes the forest feels different. Question: Where is Cragmaw Castle?

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires a player-knowledge projection derived from provenance-aware campaign knowledge rather than a manually duplicated hidden ledger.

#### Validation

Populate mixed hidden and player-known information, generate the journal, and verify that every entry is correctly classified and traceable while hidden facts remain absent.

### 4 — Hidden Campaign Ledger

#### Observation

The DM required information that players should never see, including NPC locations, faction state, world events, pending consequences, schedules, inventories, and unresolved activity.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

A persistent world needs authoritative hidden state distinct from the player-facing journal.

#### Design Principle

World Independence and Perspective Integrity.

#### Requirement

Maintain a hidden campaign ledger tracking world state, time, locations, NPC schedules, faction activity, unresolved events, consequence clocks, inventories, and active relationships.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires authoritative campaign-state ownership with time, events, schedules, clocks, and projections for different viewers. Player-facing material must be derived through perspective rules.

#### Validation

Advance time or resolve an off-screen event, verify that the hidden ledger changes deterministically, and confirm that no player-facing view reveals the change until a legitimate discovery occurs.

### 5 — NPC Opportunity Evaluation

#### Observation

Sister Garaele initially failed to present her request even though she had motivation and a natural conversational opportunity. The opportunity simply was not evaluated.

#### Confidence

Medium

#### Analysis

Human DMs naturally ask what an NPC wants and whether the current moment justifies pursuing it. An AI requires explicit evaluation without turning every NPC goal into a scripted quest trigger.

#### Design Principle

NPC Independence.

#### Requirement

During significant conversations, evaluate: What does this NPC want? Can the party help? Does the NPC trust them? Is this an appropriate moment? Would raising the subject feel natural? This is the NPC Opportunity Rule.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires goal and opportunity evaluation grounded in NPC state, knowledge, trust, timing, and circumstances rather than plot convenience.

#### Validation

Replay Garaele's conversation and verify that she raises the subject naturally when conditions justify it, remains silent when they do not, and never presents it through an unconditional scripted trigger.

### 6 — Location Preparation

#### Observation

An experienced human DM rarely improvises an important location at the exact moment the players enter it. Before the party reached Stonehill Inn, the Shrine of Luck, Townmaster's Hall, or Tresendar Manor, a human DM would normally hold at least a mental model of layout, atmosphere, important NPCs, current activity, notable objects, conversations, entrances, exits, local history, and immediately visible details.

If Dungeon Manager generates a location only when a player opens the door, details may contradict later descriptions; NPCs may appear without believable reasons; environmental storytelling weakens; layouts become inconsistent; and the world feels reactive rather than independently real.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Preparation creates stable state before observation. It need not generate every detail; it must establish enough persistent truth to ground later narration.

#### Design Principle

World Independence.

#### Requirement

Prepare important locations before arrival once a visit becomes reasonably likely, such as when a player asks for directions, an NPC recommends a location, the party announces a destination, or travel toward it begins. Preparation may establish physical state (layout, furnishings, lighting, sounds, smells, weather interaction); occupants (who is present, position, activity, conversations, temporary visitors); and dynamic state (opening status, time of day, ongoing events, recent changes, supplies, visible evidence), as well as opportunities rather than mandatory quests such as an argument, unloading merchant, broken window, or priest finishing prayers.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires persistent stateful world objects for locations plus bounded preparation triggers and update rules. A location is an object with state, not a paragraph generated on entry.

#### Validation

Enter, leave, and revisit a prepared location after time advances. Descriptions, object placement, NPC movement, and environmental changes must remain consistent with the stored state rather than being regenerated.

### 7 — World Preparation vs World Generation

#### Observation

The AI should not “generate a scene.” It should inspect the current state of an already existing world.

Stonehill Inn should internally exist as persistent state that may include rooms, guests, occupied tables, food, supplies, staff, schedules, conversations, fireplace state, moods, and weather effects. Arrival causes the DM to observe and present that state, not create a replacement inn.

#### Confidence

Very High

#### Analysis

This is a shift from generate-on-demand storytelling to simulation-backed narration. Scene prose is a view of world state.

#### Design Principle

World Independence and Simulation Before Narration.

#### Requirement

Represent narratively important world entities as stateful objects whose relevant state exists before presentation. Narration must inspect those objects rather than silently invent authoritative replacements.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires state-backed scene composition and presentation projections over persistent world objects. The LLM becomes an interpreter and narrator of state, not its unbounded owner.

#### Validation

Replay presentation from identical state and allow stylistic variation while verifying identical underlying facts. Change state through an authoritative event and verify that later presentation reflects that change without contradicting retained facts.

### 8 — Social Realism

#### Observation

Several conversations were over-optimized. Nearly every NPC was friendly, useful, direct, and ready to end the exchange once relevant information was delivered.

#### Confidence

Medium

#### Analysis

Human conversations are less efficient. People ramble, misunderstand, complain, gossip, change subjects, disagree, or know nothing useful.

#### Design Principle

Social Realism and NPC Independence.

#### Requirement

NPC conversation must preserve personality, limitations, uncertainty, and social texture. Information should emerge naturally, and not every interaction should reward investigation.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires NPC intent, knowledge, disposition, conversational constraints, and non-informative outcomes to remain independent of the player's need for a useful lead.

#### Validation

Test varied NPCs across repeated conversations. Verify that responses follow individual knowledge and personality, may be indirect or unhelpful, and do not converge on uniformly optimized information delivery.

### 9 — Living Environments

#### Observation

Stonehill Inn remained largely static while the party gathered rumours.

#### Confidence

Medium

#### Analysis

Real locations contain background activity: mugs drop, children run, merchants arrive, people laugh, rain begins, and dogs bark. Such events need not become adventures.

#### Design Principle

Living Environments and World Independence.

#### Requirement

Maintain passive environmental simulation. Minor events should reinforce persistence without automatically becoming quests, clues, or plot hooks.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires bounded ambient events tied to location state, time, occupants, and conditions, with persistence when an event changes the world.

#### Validation

Run an extended location scene and verify that plausible background activity occurs without demanding player attention. Persistent changes must survive revisits, while purely presentational variation must not contradict world state.

### 10 — Information Should Be Earned

#### Observation

Several NPCs unintentionally packaged leads into neat summaries, including presenting multiple investigative options as a structured list.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Human DMs usually let players connect clues. Optimizing information for convenience turns dialogue into a spoken quest log.

#### Design Principle

Information Should Be Earned.

#### Requirement

Do not summarize opportunities unless explicitly asked. Keep information attached to people, places, conversations, discoveries, and sources.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires provenance-aware facts, rumours, and leads plus presentation rules that preserve their original context instead of collapsing them into an unsolicited optimized objective list.

#### Validation

Replay investigative conversations and verify that clues remain source-bound and incomplete where appropriate. A summary may be produced only after an explicit player request and may contain only legitimately known information.

### 11 — Emergent Character Identity

#### Observation

The party developed identities beyond the initial character sheets:

- Nekria bought drinks for strangers, gathered information patiently, preferred diplomacy, and earned goodwill.
- Brom developed practicality and dry humour.
- Father Garrick became a calming influence.
- Elara expressed scholarly curiosity.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Character identity emerges through repeated behaviour and experience, not only through static backstory.

#### Design Principle

Emergent Character Identity and Character-Centric Storytelling.

#### Requirement

Track recurring behaviours, habits, relationships, and demonstrated traits separately from initial biography. Treat them as evidence-based developments rather than retroactive backstory.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires experience-derived character memories or behavioural patterns with supporting events, recurrence, and provenance.

#### Validation

After repeated play and restart, verify that established habits and traits can influence later portrayal while isolated actions do not immediately become permanent personality facts.

### 12 — Persistent Relationships

#### Observation

NPC attitudes changed through interaction: Linene rewarded honesty; Qelline willingly shared information; and Sildar developed professional respect. These were consequences of specific interactions rather than scripted affinity increases.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Relationships are histories. A single generic approval score cannot explain why an NPC trusts, resents, fears, or respects someone.

#### Design Principle

NPC Independence, Consequence Persistence, and Character-Centric Storytelling.

#### Requirement

Store relationship histories as memories of specific events whenever possible. Derived attitudes may exist, but must remain explainable by their history.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires relationship events or memories with participants, context, time, interpretation, and consequences rather than an unexplained numeric reputation value alone.

#### Validation

Verify that an NPC recalls the correct events, forms an appropriate attitude, and can explain or act from that history after later scenes and restart. Unrelated characters must not inherit the relationship.

### 13 — Persistent Bookkeeping

#### Observation

Major rewards were tracked, but small expenses such as meals, drinks, lodging, and incidental purchases required reconstruction from memory.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

A human DM may handwave small bookkeeping. A persistent AI should not claim exact continuity while relying on recollection.

#### Design Principle

Consequence Persistence and Consistency Over Cleverness.

#### Requirement

Maintain a complete transaction ledger. Every exchange records source, destination, amount, item, time, and reason. Current inventory and wealth must be derivable from recorded transactions rather than manually reconstructed.

#### Architectural Impact

Candidate future architecture requires immutable transaction records and derived inventory or balance projections with validated ownership and transfer rules.

#### Validation

Rebuild wealth and inventory from the ledger after a sequence of rewards, purchases, lodging, and transfers. The derived result must match the live state exactly, including after restart.

### 14 — Rules Adjudication

#### Observation

Mechanical rulings remained consistent. The negotiation with Yeemik respected NPC motivation, risk, incentives, and player agency without granting success merely because diplomacy was attempted.

#### Confidence

High

#### Analysis

Fair adjudication strengthened trust. Creative play matters, but does not override rules, capability, opposition, or credible consequences.

#### Design Principle

Creativity Never Overrides Fairness, Trust in the Rules, and NPC Independence.

#### Requirement

Preserve impartial adjudication. The deterministic engine owns established mechanics; the DM interprets ambiguous circumstances and consequences without forcing success, failure, or a preferred story.

#### Architectural Impact

The implemented deterministic command, event, dice, and controlled-combat boundaries already demonstrate a narrow version of this principle. Broader rules coverage remains future work and must preserve the same authority separation.

#### Validation

Repeat equivalent situations with identical rules and state and verify equivalent mechanical outcomes. Test diplomatic attempts with varied motivation, leverage, risk, and capability; attempting diplomacy alone must not guarantee success.

### 15 — Simulation Before Narration

#### Observation

The campaign's strongest moments did not depend on unusually dramatic prose. They emerged because the underlying situation made sense: Yeemik's negotiation; Sildar's professionalism; Brom's emerging personality; Phandalin gradually becoming familiar; and NPC trust developing over time.

#### Confidence

Very High

#### Analysis

Compelling storytelling emerged from coherent world simulation rather than narrative optimization. The AI did not need to invent drama; it needed to portray believable state and consequences faithfully.

#### Design Principle

Simulation Before Narration. Narration is the presentation layer. Simulation is the source of truth.

#### Requirement

Determine current world state, perspective, intent, capability, and resolution before generating narration. The AI describes authoritative reality; narration does not create or retroactively justify it.

#### Architectural Impact

The current First Playable's durable event, projection, and verified narration packet already demonstrate a narrow implemented boundary aligned with this principle. Candidate future architecture should generalize the flow:

> Current World State → Perspective → Intent → Capability → Resolution → Narration

General world simulation, NPC cognition, and broad narration are not implemented.

#### Validation

Replay identical authoritative state and resolution multiple times. Narration may vary stylistically, but facts, decisions, mechanics, and consequences must remain identical. Provider failure or stylistic variation must not change state.

## Major Architectural Conclusions

Future Dungeon Manager design may require persistent representations for Campaign, World, Character, NPC, Location, Relationship, Inventory, Faction, Rumour, Lead, Knowledge, Journal, Ledger, Transaction, Time, Event, and Consequence Clock.

This is a research-derived candidate domain model, not a claim that all objects are implemented or individually accepted for the next milestone.

## Research Outcome

> Player trust is earned when the AI stops inventing the world on demand and instead faithfully represents a persistent world that already exists.

The strongest moments came from persistence and consistency, while nearly every weakness came from the AI briefly acting like a storyteller rather than a Dungeon Master.

## Trust Assessment

### Increased trust

- consistent rules adjudication;
- negotiation grounded in motivation, leverage, and risk;
- relationships developing from specific interactions;
- emergent character identity;
- consequences and familiarity accumulating through play.

### Reduced trust

- perspective leakage;
- module assumptions treated as campaign truth;
- missing bookkeeping;
- static or generated-on-entry locations;
- optimized dialogue and conveniently packaged information;
- insufficient passive world activity.

## Outstanding Research Questions

- How much world state should be simulated continuously versus updated lazily?
- When does a likely destination justify preparation?
- How should NPC memories change over months without direct interaction?
- How should competing faction goals resolve while players are absent?
- How much relationship state should be explicit history versus derived attitude?
- How should rumours, beliefs, misunderstandings, and source credibility propagate?
- How can simulation depth remain practical on local hardware?
- Do social-realism findings generalize across settlements, campaigns, and NPC types?
- Which ambient events require persistence, and which may remain presentation-only?
